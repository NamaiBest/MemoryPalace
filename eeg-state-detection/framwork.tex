\section{Notation and setup}

A single trial is a matrix
\[
X \in \Real^{C \times T},
\]
$C$ channels by $T$ time samples, already band-pass filtered and
baseline-corrected. Rows are electrodes, columns are samples. Write
$X_{i\cdot}$ for the $i$th channel's time course.

A calibration set is $\{(X_n, y_n)\}_{n=1}^{N}$ with $y_n \in \{0,1\}$.
All quantities below that are \emph{learned} are fitted on training
folds only and then frozen; applying them to a test trial uses the
stored values.

\section{Covariance}

\subsection{Definition}

Assume each row is mean-centred, $\sum_t X_{it} = 0$. The sample
covariance is
\[
\Sigma \;=\; \frac{1}{T-1} X X^\top \;\in\; \Real^{C \times C},
\qquad
\Sigma_{ij} \;=\; \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T} X_{it} X_{jt}.
\]
The diagonal holds per-channel variance; off-diagonals hold covariation
between channel pairs.

\subsection{Two properties}

\textbf{Symmetry.} $\Sigma_{ij} = \Sigma_{ji}$, immediate from the
definition, so $\Sigma = \Sigma^\top$.

\textbf{Positive semi-definiteness.} For any $w \in \Real^C$,
\[
w^\top \Sigma w \;=\; \frac{1}{T-1}\, w^\top X X^\top w
\;=\; \frac{1}{T-1}\,\big\| X^\top w \big\|_2^2 \;\ge\; 0 .
\]
The middle expression is the variance of the mixed signal $w^\top X$,
which cannot be negative. It is strictly positive, giving
$\Sigma \in \SPD{C}$, whenever no linear combination of channels is
identically zero --- requiring $T \ge C$ and no exactly redundant
channel.

\subsection{Shrinkage}

For short windows the empirical estimator is poorly conditioned, which
makes the matrix logarithm of Section~4 numerically unstable. Use the
Ledoit--Wolf estimator
\[
\hat\Sigma \;=\; (1-\alpha)\,\Sigma \;+\; \alpha\,\frac{\Tr(\Sigma)}{C}\, I,
\qquad \alpha \in [0,1]
\]
with $\alpha$ chosen analytically. This pulls eigenvalues away from
zero, and hence their logarithms away from $-\infty$.

\subsection{Time invariance --- why this is not yet enough}

Let $P$ be any $T \times T$ permutation. Then
\[
(XP)(XP)^\top = X P P^\top X^\top = X X^\top ,
\]
so $\Sigma$ is invariant to reordering the time samples. It encodes
\emph{spatial} structure only. A response defined by its shape and
latency is invisible to it, which is what Section~3 repairs.

\section{Spatial filtering and prototype augmentation}

\subsection{xDAWN}

A spatial filter $w \in \Real^C$ produces a virtual channel $w^\top X$.
Let $\bar{X}_1 \in \Real^{C\times T}$ be the average of the class-1
trials (the evoked response, estimated by least squares under a
superposition model), and let $\Sigma_{\text{sig}}$ and
$\Sigma_{\text{tot}}$ be the covariances of $\bar{X}_1$ and of the raw
data. xDAWN maximises the Rayleigh quotient
\[
w^\star \;=\; \arg\max_{w}\;
\frac{w^\top \Sigma_{\text{sig}} \, w}{w^\top \Sigma_{\text{tot}}\, w},
\]
whose stationary points solve the generalized eigenvalue problem
$\Sigma_{\text{sig}} w = \lambda \, \Sigma_{\text{tot}} w$. Keeping the
top $K$ eigenvectors gives $W \in \Real^{C \times K}$.

Two consequences worth stating. Weights may be negative, which lets the
filter subtract channels carrying shared noise --- something no
single-electrode choice can do. And hard selection is in the feasible
set ($w = e_i$), so a learned filter cannot do worse than hand-picking
except through estimation error.

\subsection{Augmentation}

Stack the filtered prototype on the filtered trial:
\[
\tilde{X} \;=\;
\begin{bmatrix} W^\top \bar{X}_1 \\[2pt] W^\top X \end{bmatrix}
\in \Real^{2K \times T},
\qquad
\Sigma \;=\; \frac{1}{T-1}\,\tilde{X}\tilde{X}^\top \in \SPD{2K}.
\]
In block form,
\[
\Sigma \;=\;
\begin{bmatrix}
\Sigma_{\bar{X}\bar{X}} & \Sigma_{\bar{X}X} \\[2pt]
\Sigma_{X\bar{X}} & \Sigma_{XX}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The off-diagonal block has entries
\[
\big(\Sigma_{\bar{X}X}\big)_{ij}
= \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{t} \big(W^\top \bar{X}_1\big)_{it}\,\big(W^\top X\big)_{jt},
\]
a correlation of the trial against a template with fixed time structure
--- a matched filter. Permuting the trial's samples destroys it. The
timing information lost in Section~2.4 re-enters here.

The diagonal block $\Sigma_{XX}$ carries the trial's own noise level,
so the classifier can weigh template match against the noise it sat in.

\paragraph{Oscillatory variant.} If the target is a sustained rhythm
rather than a time-locked response, drop the augmentation: band-pass to
the band of interest and take $\Sigma = \frac{1}{T-1}XX^\top$ directly.
Everything from Section~4 onward is unchanged. Time invariance is then
a feature, not a defect.

\section{The manifold}

\subsection{Why not Euclidean}

$\SPD{d}$ is an open convex cone inside the vector space of symmetric
matrices, not a subspace: it is not closed under negation or
subtraction. Treating it as flat distorts results. With
$A=\operatorname{diag}(1,4)$ and $B=\operatorname{diag}(4,1)$, both of
determinant $4$,
\[
\tfrac{1}{2}(A+B) = \operatorname{diag}(2.5,\,2.5), \quad \det = 6.25,
\]
spread that neither input had. Covariance composes multiplicatively:
scaling a signal by $a$ scales its covariance by $a^2$. Arithmetic
averaging is a rule for additive quantities.

\subsection{Matrix functions}

For $M \in \SPD{d}$ with eigendecomposition $M = V \Lambda V^\top$,
$\Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_d)$,
$\lambda_i > 0$, define
\[
M^{p} = V \Lambda^{p} V^\top, \qquad
\log M = V (\log \Lambda) V^\top, \qquad
\exp S = V (\exp \Lambda) V^\top .
\]
Eigenvectors are untouched; the scalar function acts on eigenvalues.
Positive definiteness is exactly the condition making $\log M$ real and
unique. Note this is \emph{not} the entrywise logarithm.

\subsection{Affine-invariant metric}

\[
\delta(A,B) \;=\; \big\| \log\!\big(B^{-1/2} A B^{-1/2}\big) \big\|_F
\;=\; \Big(\textstyle\sum_{i} \log^2 \lambda_i \Big)^{1/2},
\]
where $\lambda_i$ solve $\det(A - \lambda B)=0$. For any invertible
$G \in \Real^{d\times d}$,
\[
\delta\big(GAG^\top,\; GBG^\top\big) \;=\; \delta(A,B).
\]
Volume conduction and re-referencing are linear maps on the sensors, so
this geometry is blind to an entire class of nuisance transformations.

\subsection{Geometric mean}

\[
M \;=\; \arg\min_{M \in \SPD{d}} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \delta^2(M, \Sigma_n).
\]
No closed form for $d>1$. Fixed-point iteration, from
$M^{(0)} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_n \Sigma_n$:
\[
S^{(k)} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
   \log\!\big(M^{(k)\,-1/2} \Sigma_n M^{(k)\,-1/2}\big),
\qquad
M^{(k+1)} = M^{(k)\,1/2} \exp\!\big(S^{(k)}\big) M^{(k)\,1/2},
\]
stopping when $\|S^{(k)}\|_F < \varepsilon$. The mean is characterised
by its tangent-space mean being zero.

\section{Tangent space}

\subsection{The map}

Fix the reference $M$ from Section 4.4. For a trial covariance $\Sigma$:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Whiten.}
$\tilde\Sigma = M^{-1/2}\, \Sigma\, M^{-1/2}$.

The two-sided form is forced: a linear map $x \mapsto Gx$ on the data
sends $\Sigma \mapsto G\Sigma G^\top$, since covariance is a quadratic
form. With $G = M^{-1/2}$ (symmetric), this is the sandwich. One-sided
$M^{-1}\Sigma$ has the same eigenvalues but is not symmetric and leaves
$\SPD{d}$.

Check: $\Sigma = M \Rightarrow \tilde\Sigma = I$. The eigenvalues of
$\tilde\Sigma$ are the generalized eigenvalues of $(\Sigma, M)$: the
ratio of this trial's variance to typical, per direction, and so
independent of overall gain.

\item \textbf{Logarithm.} $S = \log \tilde\Sigma$.

Now $\Sigma = M \Rightarrow S = 0$: the reference is the origin.
A direction with $4\times$ typical variance maps to $+\log 4$, with
$\tfrac{1}{4}\times$ to $-\log 4$. Multiplicative becomes additive.

\item \textbf{Vectorize.} With $\mathcal{I} = \{(i,j): i \le j\}$,
\[
v = \vect(S) = \big[\, S_{11},\; \sqrt{2}\,S_{12},\; \dots,\;
   S_{22},\; \dots \,\big] \in \Real^{d(d+1)/2}.
\]
The $\sqrt{2}$ on off-diagonals is required for the isometry
$\|v\|_2 = \|S\|_F$, since each off-diagonal entry appears twice in
$\|S\|_F^2$. Hence $\|v\|_2 = \delta(\Sigma, M)$: Euclidean distance on
the chart equals manifold distance from the reference.
\end{enumerate}

For $K=3$ filters, $d = 2K = 6$ and $\dim v = 21$, against $C \cdot T$
raw features --- the dimensionality reduction that makes the model
trainable at a few hundred trials.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The chart is exact at $M$ and degrades with distance, like a flat map
of a curved surface: accurate for a city, distorting for a hemisphere.
Since $M$ is the mean of the training data, most trials sit near the
point of tangency.

\section{Classifier}

Standardize each coordinate using training-fold statistics only:
$z = (v - \mu) \oslash \sigma$. The chart is already centred near zero
by construction, so this is about scale: diagonal entries are
log-variance ratios, off-diagonals carry the $\sqrt{2}$ weight, and the
$\ell_2$ penalty is not scale invariant.

\[
\hat\beta = \arg\min_{\beta,\beta_0}
\; \sum_{n=1}^{N} c_{y_n} \log\!\Big(1 + e^{-\tilde{y}_n(\beta^\top z_n + \beta_0)}\Big)
\;+\; \frac{1}{2C_{\mathrm{reg}}}\|\beta\|_2^2 ,
\]
with $\tilde{y}_n \in \{-1,+1\}$ and $c_{y}$ inverse class frequencies.

\textbf{$\ell_2$, not $\ell_1$.} Covariance coordinates are strongly
correlated; $\ell_1$ retains an arbitrary member of a correlated group
and zeroes the rest, giving coefficients unstable across folds. There
is no sparsity to gain at $21$ features.

\textbf{$C_{\mathrm{reg}}$ must be retuned at every training size.} The
optimum depends on $N$. Fixing it while sweeping $N$ measures a curve
about a mis-set hyperparameter, not a learning curve. Use an inner
cross-validation loop.

\textbf{Score, not decision.} The output
$s = \sigma(\hat\beta^\top z + \hat\beta_0)$ is used to rank candidate
windows. Deployment class imbalance ($\approx 1{:}60$) is far from
calibration imbalance ($\approx 1{:}5$), so $s$ is badly calibrated as
a probability; ranking requires only monotonicity in the likelihood
ratio, which is preserved.

\section{Summary}

\paragraph{Fit (once per subject per session).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item Filter, epoch, reject artifacts.
\item Learn $W$ and $\bar{X}_1$ from labeled trials.
\item Per trial: augment, estimate $\Sigma_n$ with shrinkage.
\item Iterate for $M$; store $M^{-1/2}$.
\item Map all trials to $v_n$; fit $\mu,\sigma$ and $\hat\beta$,
      tuning $C_{\mathrm{reg}}$ by inner cross-validation.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Score.}
Same $W$, $\bar{X}_1$, $M^{-1/2}$, $\mu$, $\sigma$, $\hat\beta$ ---
all frozen. Whiten, log, vectorize, standardize, predict, rank.

\paragraph{Leakage.} Recomputing $M$ (or $W$, $\bar{X}_1$, $\mu$,
$\sigma$) on test data both leaks labels and places train and test
features in different coordinate systems, voiding the fitted weights.

\paragraph{Verification.} The following were checked numerically
against an independent implementation and all hold to $\sim\!10^{-10}$:
$M^{-1/2} M M^{-1/2} = I$; $\vect(\log(M^{-1/2}MM^{-1/2})) = 0$;
$\|v\|_2 = \delta(\Sigma,M)$; affine invariance under random invertible
$G$; and $\frac{1}{N}\sum_n v_n = 0$ at the geometric mean.
